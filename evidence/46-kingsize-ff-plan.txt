 Then here's where we actually are — the unlock is now computable rather than speculative, and I found two traps on the way.

 The FF map is confirmed on your die. Reconstructing every chain-1 floorsweep from the row image I banked reproduces the BAR0 values I measured independently — FBIO 0xC0330C, FBPA 0xC0330C, FBP 0x852, ROP_L2 0xC0330C, PES 0x3803F, GPC 0x23, NVLINK 0x7,
 SYS_PIPE 0xFE, TPC masks ff ff 01 00 00 ff 01 01 = 37 TPC = 74 SM. That's the differential cross-SKU mapping Kate's researcher built, re-derived from one card and checked against a second, unrelated read path.

 Trap one — the rir tool is not safe to use here. Its --write-rir reads and writes FUSEADDR 0..7, and on this card those addresses return 0x53557c3d — Kate's control-row value, with rows coming in identical PRI/RED pairs. So its "RIR" aperture is
 addressing main rows 0–3, the one region with "basically no remedy," not the TSMC repair macro. I won't run --write-rir against this card, and I'd be careful with it on any card.

 Trap two — blocks are shared, so records must be merged. FBPA and FBIO both live partly in block 6, and block 8 holds both FBPA bits and GPC. Emit one record per word means the later record silently reverts the earlier, because last-wins is per block,
 not per option word. My first composition did exactly that — it would have emitted block6 = 0x6601 for FBIO immediately after block6 = 0x0601 for FBPA, undoing the memory unlock. The planner has to merge all edits per block into one record.

 Two things genuinely still unknown, and I'd rather name them than paper over them. SENSE_CTRL's FUSECTRL value isn't in any public header — Kate's trick (re-sense restores every control-row option except DSOV, so ENABLE_FUSE_PROGRAM returns to 1 while
 SBFE can then be shadowed to 0, plus clearing OPT_SECURE_FUSE_WDATA_WR_SECURE at 0x0082048C to open the FUSEWDATA PLM) is the arming path, but the encoding we derive by observation at Goldilocks. And NVDEC/NVJPG/OFA have no published FF location —
 they're not in chain 1's known ranges, so without a second SKU's row dump I can't plan them honestly; I'd leave them out of the first burn rather than guess at chain-2 blocks.

 One correction to my own earlier pessimism worth flagging: Gen3 and Gen4 are plain FF bits (chain 2 block 39 for Gen3 — bit 1 or 9 still ambiguous — block 47 bit 3 for Gen4, block 49 bits 6/7 for boot policy), and she reports them training and fully
 functional. Since your card already has the decoupling-cap mod, Gen4 is in scope after memory and SMs are banked, though it pays nothing on pve1's x8 slot.
